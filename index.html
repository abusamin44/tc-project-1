    Family Goals

    Moorning Time

    On Eid morning, we wake up early, take a purifying bath (ghusl), wear clean or new clothes, and apply perfume. Then we to the mosque or Eidgah to offer the special Eid prayer. After prayer, we greet others with “Eid Mubarak,” showing love and unity. Then, we return home to perform the Qurbani (sacrifice) in remembrance of Prophet Ibrahim’s devotion. The meat is divided into three parts—for family, relatives or friends, and the poor—spreading joy and care throughout the community.

    Children views

    On Eid day, children joyfully dress in bright new clothes and line up to receive Eidi—cash, sweets, or small gifts—from elders after touching their feet as a sign of respect. They then go house-to-house, visiting relatives and enjoying traditional treats while strengthening family bonds with greetings and laughter.

    Visitores

    On Eid-ul-Adha, when relatives visit, they are warmly welcomed with smiles and greetings. Traditionally, hosts serve fresh beef curry with hot parathas or ruti. Guests are offered sweets or drinks too. As part of the celebration, a portion of the Qurbani meat is gifted to them, showing love, hospitality, and the spirit of sharing that defines the occasion..
